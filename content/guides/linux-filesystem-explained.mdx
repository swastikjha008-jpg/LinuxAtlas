## Overview

Everything in Linux lives under a single root, `/`, unlike the multiple drive letters used elsewhere. Standard directories like `/etc`, `/var`, and `/home` each have a defined purpose under the Filesystem Hierarchy Standard (FHS), so once you know the convention, any distro's layout is predictable.

## Key directories

- `/etc` — system-wide configuration files, almost always plain text
- `/var` — variable data: logs, caches, spool files, databases
- `/home` — per-user data and configuration
- `/usr` — the bulk of installed software and its resources
- `/tmp` — temporary files, typically cleared on reboot
- `/proc` and `/sys` — virtual filesystems exposing kernel and process state, not real files on disk

## Why it matters

Knowing this layout means you always know where to look — a misbehaving service's config is in `/etc`, its logs are in `/var/log`, and its binary is somewhere under `/usr`. That predictability is one of Linux's most underrated conveniences once it clicks.
